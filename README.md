# Replication Laboratory #1 — Evaluation Design Matters

**ANLY 735 — Research Seminar in Predictive AI**

## Laboratory Question

> **Does changing the evaluation design change estimated predictive performance—and does it change which model appears stronger?**

Predictive performance is estimated under an evaluation design. In this laboratory, you will investigate whether changing that design changes the evidence available to a researcher.

You will analyze the **same prediction problem** using the same dataset, predictors, models, and evaluation metrics under two conditions:

1. **Random evaluation** — observations are divided into training and testing data without preserving temporal order.
2. **Temporally structured evaluation** — earlier observations are used for training and later observations are reserved for testing.

Your task is not to obtain a predetermined result. Your task is to determine what the evidence supports.

> **Do not chase identical numbers. Investigate reproducibility.**

---

## Research Workflow

```text
CLAIM → REPLICATE → COMPARE → DIAGNOSE → EXTEND
```

During the comparison, use the **FAIR Model Comparison Audit**:

- **F — Frame:** What exactly is being compared?
- **A — Align:** Are the model conditions comparable?
- **I — Inspect:** What do RMSE and MAE show?
- **R — Review:** Does the conclusion change when evaluation design changes?

## Dataset

This laboratory uses the **UCI Bike Sharing Dataset**, containing hourly bike-rental observations from 2011–2012.

**Prediction outcome:** `cnt` — total hourly bike rentals.

The analysis intentionally excludes:

- `instant` — identifier;
- `dteday` — used to establish chronology rather than as a direct predictor;
- `casual` and `registered` — components of the outcome `cnt`.

The scripts acquire the original dataset automatically if `data/hour.csv` is not already present.

Dataset documentation:  
https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/275/bike+sharing+dataset

## What the Analysis Produces

Your selected script will:

1. acquire or access the dataset reproducibly;
2. establish chronological order;
3. define the prediction problem;
4. fit Ridge Regression and Random Forest models;
5. evaluate both models under random evaluation;
6. evaluate both models under temporal evaluation;
7. calculate RMSE and MAE;
8. display model rankings; and
9. save the generated results to:

```text
analysis/lab01_results.csv
```

The script produces **evidence**. It does not provide your scholarly interpretation.

## Laboratory Standard

A successful laboratory does more than report which model has the lowest error.

Ask:

> **What changed when the evaluation design changed?**

Then ask:

> **Which parts of my conclusion survived that change?**

Finally:

> **What claim does the evidence permit me to make—and where must that claim stop?**
